# GDPR email retention for a small club mailing list

Status: web reached (not `unverified`), but several sources are secondary. Not legal advice.

## Question
Small volunteer club, members mostly EU, list stores email addresses. (1) Is there a fixed maximum retention under GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) / Art. 17, and what do regulators say for (a) unconfirmed double opt-in signups, (b) a member's email after leaving? (2) Can any trace of a departed person be kept (suppression record, legal retention)? Is hashing an email anonymisation? (3) Recommended defaults.

## Answer
1. **No fixed maximum period.** Art. 5(1)(e) is purpose-based: identifiable data kept no longer than necessary; the controller must set and document the period. Art. 17 adds erasure on request/when no longer necessary. A Finnish DPA case (Verkkokauppa) faulted a controller for not defining retention periods and deleting only on request.
   - (a) Unconfirmed signups: I found no regulator naming a number of days. The purpose (obtaining confirmation) is exhausted quickly, so a short, documented window is expected. The Italian Garante treats double opt-in as a minimum safeguard (decision 330/2025, per secondary source).
   - (b) Departed members: CNIL says that once someone is no longer a member, keeping their data must be justified by purposes other than managing membership (disputes proof, tax, statistics), with defined periods, automatic purge and restricted-access intermediate archive (primary, read). Secondary French sources quote "3 years after end of membership" and "1-2 years" for contact data; not confirmed as CNIL figures. Gironde FAQ: association fixes the duration itself by usefulness.
2. **Traces.** Yes, narrowly. The ICO (UK, not EU, but the clearest text) says to put people who object to marketing on a suppression/"do not contact" list rather than delete entirely, used only to honour the objection (secondary summary of ICO pages; ICO storage-limitation URL returned 404, so not read directly). Legal basis is Art. 6(1)(c)/(f) for honouring the objection/erasure; keep it minimal and say so in the privacy notice. Accounting/tax records (e.g. donation/dues receipts) follow national law (secondary French guide: accounting 10 yrs; I did not verify; German/Dutch rules not researched). Do not keep the full member profile "just in case".
   - **Hashing is not anonymisation.** An unkeyed hash of an email is deterministic and matchable against candidate address lists, so it is pseudonymised and still personal data (Recital 26; ICO; FTC 2024 per secondary sources; EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 on Pseudonymisation, which I could only confirm as a consultation draft from 17 Jan-14 Mar 2025; its final status and its hashing text were not read; AEPD/EDPS joint hashing guidance exists, not read). Practical consequence: a hash-only suppression entry is still personal data, but is a legitimate data-minimised form of suppression. A keyed hash (HMAC with secret key held separately) is stronger; still not anonymous.
3. **Defaults** (my recommendation, not regulator-mandated):

| Case | Default | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Unconfirmed double opt-in | Delete after 7 days (max 30); no reminder loop beyond one | Medium (no regulator figure; reasoning from purpose) |
| Departed member email/profile | Delete within 30-90 days of leaving/request; immediate on Art. 17 request unless a documented legal obligation applies | Medium |
| Longer retention of ex-member | Only if a documented purpose (dues/accounting records, dispute) and then in restricted archive, min. fields, fixed period (e.g. up to 3 yrs for disputes; statutory period for accounts) | Low-medium (period is national law/judgment call) |
| Suppression record (opted out / erased) | Keep keyed-hash (HMAC) of email only, no name; reviewed periodically (e.g. 3-5 yrs), disclosed in privacy notice | Medium (principle well supported; duration is judgment) |
| Hash = anonymous? | No | High |
| No fixed legal maximum | True | High |

## Confidence
High: no fixed maximum; hashed email is not anonymous. Medium: suppression record is allowed if minimal; short deletion windows. Low: exact day/year numbers; national association/accounting retention law.

## What it changes in a plan
- Add a purge job: unconfirmed signups deleted after 7 days.
- On leave/unsubscribe/erasure: delete profile; write only an HMAC(email) suppression row (secret key kept outside the DB); document it in the privacy notice and a retention table.
- Do not describe hashed data as "anonymised" in code, docs or UI.
- Open item: confirm national accounting/association record rules for the club's country and check the EDPB final guidelines.
